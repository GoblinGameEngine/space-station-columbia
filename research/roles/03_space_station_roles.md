# 3. Roles on large enclosed stations: the scholarly record

Columbia is a rotating habitat of towns. The relevant literature has two parts: studies of the
**formal roles** a settlement in space needs, and studies of the **informal roles** that decide
whether isolated groups hold together.

## 3.1 Designing a space settlement's population
- **NASA SP-413, *Space Settlements: A Design Study* (1975)**, "The People in the Colony"
  ([chapter 4](https://nss.org/settlement/nasa/75SummerStudy/Chapt4.html)).
  - A 10,000-person colony grows from about **2,000 construction workers**, each bringing one to
    three family members.
  - Compared with the 1970 US census, it has about **10% more men**, and a population shifted
    **into ages 21–44**, away from under 20 and over 45. More married women work.
  - "**61 percent of the workforce, or nearly 44 percent of the population** of the initial colony
    would be producing for export." At an early stage the workforce is about 3,200 of 4,300.
  - The human-needs chapter adds that a 10,000-person community cannot provide the full range of
    services a city of 100,000–200,000 can, so trade and electronic services fill the gap (psychology 03 §3.3).
  - It proposes **three social organisations**: hierarchical, individualistic and heterogenistic.
  - **For us:**
    - settlements have a **working-age bulge**, founders' families and an **export economy**
      (station industry);
    - some Earth roles are thin or absent, so one person **wears several hats**: role strain, and
      prominence;
    - the three social organisations shape which civic roles exist.
- ***Living Aloft: Human Requirements for Extended Spaceflight*** (Connors, Harrison & Akins,
  NASA SP-483, 1985) ([NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19850024459)).
  - The definitive NASA review of performance, well-being and **social organisation** in space. It
    emphasises crew size, diversity and duration, all of which grow in a settlement.
  - Its themes: formal leadership plus informal structure, status and role clarity, privacy, and
    crew–ground relations.
- **Stuster, *Bold Endeavors: Lessons from Polar and Space Exploration* (1996)**, 15 years of
  NASA and DoD research on submarines, Antarctic stations, polar expeditions and voyages
  ([USNI](https://www.usni.org/press/books/bold-endeavors); [summary](https://ecolloq.gsfc.nasa.gov/archive/2007-Fall/announce.stuster.html)).
  - What keeps people well: **communal meals**, contact with family, a place to retreat, games and
    films, a feasible and meaningful workload, **clear rules for communal living**, and
    understanding from planners and colleagues.
  - Its chapter on **food** is used in culinary programmes. **The cook is a morale role.**

## 3.2 Informal roles decide whether a group holds
- **Johnson, Boster & Palinkas (2003), South Pole winter-over crews.**
  - **Coherent** networks (one core and a periphery) went with **group consensus that critical
    informal roles were filled**, above all **expressive leadership**.
  - **Factionalised** networks went with **no consensus on critical roles**, above all the
    **instrumental leader** ([summary](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1213007822580813831)).
- **Johnson's four years of Antarctic fieldwork.** Crews need **clowns, leaders, buddies,
  storytellers, peacemakers and counsellors**. "If you have the right combination the group does
  very well. If you don't, the group does very badly". The pattern held at US, Russian, Polish,
  Chinese and Indian bases.
  - At one South Pole station, **the clown was the most central figure**, bridging the scientists
    and the trades crew (plumbers, electricians, mechanics).
  - Less effective groups lacked a clown or other informal leaders
    ([Nautilus](https://nautil.us/why-we-need-court-jesters-in-space-237446); [Guardian report via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-asian-age/20190217/281977493890361)).
- **Palinkas, "On the Ice"** (2000; local copy in `reference/roles/`).
  - One South Pole winter crew formed **three cliques named for where they spent leisure time: the
    "Biomed" group, the "Library" group and the "Bar" group**, five people each.
  - Crews with a **clique structure** reported **significantly more depression, anxiety, anger,
    fatigue and confusion** than crews with a **core–periphery** structure.
  - In earlier Antarctic stations, **being married** was a significant independent predictor of
    **leadership**, and military crews outscored civilians in peer ratings.
  - The **support paradox:** support is valued, but people fear being burdened by others'
    problems.
  - Behaviour in ICE environments is **seasonal, situational, social and salutogenic**.
- **McMurdo Station** ([McMurdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMurdo_Station); Palinkas).
  - About **1,000 people in summer and under 200 in winter**.
  - Several organisations live side by side: NSF administrators, grant-funded scientists, military
    air crews, and the **contractor support staff** (cooks, maintenance, IT, heavy equipment, fire,
    medical, logistics).
  - Historically, social spaces were **segregated by rank**. Law enforcement is a **US Marshal**.
  - The **science / support divide** is a class line, like Middletown's.
- **Biosphere 2, first closure (1991–93)** ([Biosphere 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_2)).
  - Eight crew **split into two factions before the halfway point** over research priorities versus
    mission management and closure. There were public accusations of smuggled supplies and
    tampered data.
  - Yet: "I don't like some of them, but we were a hell of a team" (Walford).
  - Factions can coexist with functioning; the outside (management, press) becomes a target.
- **Kanas; Mars-500.** Tension is **displaced outward** onto mission control and management
  (psychology 03 §3.2). On a station, that means **the administration role** absorbs blame.

## 3.3 What this means for Columbia's roles
1. **Essential-systems roles exist here and not in Muncie:** air, water, power, food production,
   hull, radiation, EVA, docking, traffic and recycling. They are **high-stakes**: their holders'
   mistakes are `fatal_imprudence` and `community_crisis` arcs. Their prestige is high but they
   are often short-staffed (role strain), and they hold **tight norms** (`W.tight`).
2. **A science/tech class and a support/trades class**, as at McMurdo and Middletown: a homophily
   and rivalry line, and the gap the **clown** or **broker** bridges.
3. **Administration and "the station"** stand in for mission control, the target of displaced
   tension.
4. **Informal roles are a settlement-level need.** Each settlement gets `W.cohesion` from whether
   its critical informal roles are filled: task leader, heart leader, clown, peacemaker,
   counsellor, storyteller. When they are missing, cliques form around the settlement's leisure
   places (Biomed/Library/Bar), and mood and stress worsen (Palinkas). **The player can fill a
   vacant informal role**, and story hooks come from exactly this.
5. **Food and the shared meal matter** (Stuster): the cook, the canteen and the communal table
   are morale roles and places.
6. **Several hats.** A small settlement's doctor is also the coroner and the counsellor; its
   mechanic fixes the air plant too. People carry more `RL` entries, with more strain and more
   prominence.
7. **Arrival status** (psychology 03: `C.origin`): founders, first generation, recent arrivals and
   **contract workers**. McMurdo's seasonal contractors and SP-413's worry about incentive-lured
   settlers make the contract worker, temporary and less committed, a distinct role.
