# Cross-sections and road rules

## Lanes and shoulders (AASHTO and state DOT practice)
- Lanes are **12 ft (3.65 m)** on high-speed or high-volume roads, and **10–11 ft (3.0–3.35 m)** on urban streets and local roads.
- **Rural shoulders** are **4–8 ft (1.2–2.4 m)**, paved or gravel by volume. Low-volume gravel roads may have shoulders under 4 ft.
- Edge lines are normally omitted where a curb defines the edge.

## Urban streets: curb and gutter
- A barrier curb is **6 in (150 mm)** high, with a **gutter pan 1–2 ft (0.3–0.6 m)** wide at a 2–5 % cross-fall toward the flow line.
- Integral curb-and-gutter is standard on town streets. Mountable (rolled) curbs are used in some subdivisions.

## Intersection control (MUTCD)
- A **two-way stop** on the minor road where it meets a through road.
- **All-way stop** when the major approaches carry 300 or more units/hour and the minor 200 or more, for 8 hours of a typical day.
- **Signals** at higher volumes. In towns under 10,000 people, or above 40 mph, 70 % of those volumes suffice.
- **Stop lines** sit about 4 ft (1.2 m) ahead of the crosswalk.

## Speed
- Statutory limits:

  | Where | US | Europe |
  |---|---|---|
  | Residential / urban | 25 mph (40 km/h) | 50 km/h |
  | Rural | 55 mph (90 km/h) | 80–100 km/h |

- The UK uses 30 mph in lit built-up areas.
