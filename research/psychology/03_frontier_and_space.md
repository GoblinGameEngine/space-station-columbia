# 3. Frontier and space: what settling, isolation and a built world do to people

Space Station Columbia is both a **frontier** (settled by choice, far from the old world, young
institutions) and an **isolated, confined, extreme (ICE) environment**. It is a rotating habitat
where everything is built and the outside is lethal. Both literatures give **settlement-level
culture tokens** (prefix `W.`, shared by everyone in a settlement) and **personal adaptation
tokens** (prefix `C.`).

## 3.1 The frontier
- **Rugged individualism: selection plus exposure.**
  - Bazzi, Fiszbein & Gebresilasse (*Econometrica* 2020) built a county-level measure of **total
    frontier experience** (TFE, 1790–1890).
  - Counties with more TFE show more pervasive **individualism** and **opposition to
    redistribution** today. The pattern holds across the urban/rural and north/south divides.
  - The causes are both **selective migration** and a **causal effect of frontier exposure**
    ([Econometrica](https://econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2020/11/01/frontier-culture-roots-and-persistence-“rugged-individualism”); [NBER w23997](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23997)).
- **Voluntary settlement: Kitayama et al. 2006, Hokkaido.**
  - Economically motivated **voluntary settlement** in harsh frontier conditions, with thin
    institutions, fosters **independent agency**: personal goal pursuit, personal choice, and
    explaining behaviour by disposition.
  - Hokkaido-born Japanese resemble North Americans more than mainland Japanese on these measures
    ([voluntary settlement hypothesis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01287/pdf)).
  - For us: people who **chose** to come to the station, and their children, lean individualist,
    and the lean persists down the generations.
- **The culture of honor: Nisbett & Cohen 1996.**
  - It arises where **herding, weak law and easily stolen wealth** make a reputation for
    retaliation valuable (the Scots-Irish settlers of the US South).
  - In experiments, insulted Southerners showed larger cortisol and testosterone jumps and more
    anger and aggression, while Northerners were amused ([summary](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/culture-honor-psychology-violence-south)).
  - For us: settlements where property is **mobile and policing thin** (remote farms and ranches,
    mining camps) develop **insult sensitivity** and reputation-guarding. This is where
    violence properly comes from (see 02 §2.4).
- **Tightness and looseness: Gelfand et al. 2011, 33 nations.**
  - Tightness is "the strength of a society's norms and the strictness of its punishments for
    deviant behavior". Ecological and historical **threats** (density, scarcity, conflict,
    disaster, disease) **tighten** a culture ([Gelfand 2011](https://michelegelfand.com/_files/ugd/6df109_28c2e5049e3a43af83e23a3c86babdab.pdf)).
  - For us: a habitat where a breach or a failed air plant kills everyone is a **threat ecology**.
    Station culture leans tight around safety norms (the authority and sanctity foundations for
    anything touching life support), even among individualists. That tension is story material.
- **Relational mobility: Thomson, Yuki et al. 2018, *PNAS*, 39 countries.**
  - Relational mobility is how easy it is to **form new relationships and leave old ones**.
  - It is high in North America, Europe and Latin America, and lower where subsistence was settled
    and interdependent (rice farming) or threats were strong. It predicts behaviour: self-disclosure,
    intimacy and similarity-seeking in friends ([record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2930518)).
  - Talhelm's **rice theory** (*Science* 2014) is the interdependence side: labour-sharing
    agriculture breeds collectivism.
  - For us: new, growing settlements (arrivals, turnover) have **high relational mobility**, so
    ties form and dissolve faster and newcomers are accepted sooner. Old, closed farming valleys
    have low mobility: strong, stable ties and a slow-to-trust welcome.
- **Legacy of industry: Obschonka et al. 2018 (*JPSP*).**
  - Regions historically dominated by large-scale coal industry still show lower C, higher N, less
    activity and lower life satisfaction. This comes through selective migration and
    socialization, and is supported by an instrumental-variable design using coalfield locations
    ([paper](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/89645/1/MPRA_paper_89645.pdf)).
  - For us: a settlement's **founding economy** leaves a personality imprint on its descendants. It
    becomes a small shift on the population's trait means, computed from the settlement record.

## 3.2 Isolated, confined, extreme environments (polar stations, spaceflight, simulations)
- **Polar expeditions: Palinkas & Suedfeld 2008, *Lancet*.**
  - Isolation, confinement and the harsh surroundings bring sleep disruption, poor concentration,
    depression and **interpersonal tension**. Together these make up the *winter-over syndrome*.
  - About **5%** need treatment.
  - There are **positive, salutogenic** effects too: growth, pride, closeness
    ([summary](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/public-health/polar-expedition-can-cause-mental-highs-and-lows-606736.html)).
- **The "third quarter phenomenon": weak evidence.**
  - Bechtel & Berning (1991) proposed a morale slump after a mission's midpoint, from largely
    anecdotal evidence.
  - Later studies found it **not typical**, with no third-quarter effect on the ISS or Mir. They
    warned of demand effects ([review](https://asma.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/amhp/92/8/article-p689.xml)).
  - For us: **do not** build a global calendar slump. At most, give susceptible individuals a
    stress curve.
- **Displacement of tension: Kanas, Shuttle–Mir.**
  - Crews "displace their anger toward controllers on the ground". Crew cohesion rose with a
    supportive commander ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/how-to-get-along-in-space-36154123/); [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040088436)).
  - **Mars-500** (520 days) agrees: "Conflicts with mission control were reported five times
    more often than conflicts among crewmembers" ([Basner et al. 2014](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/penn-medicine-520-day-simulated-mission-mars-reveals-critical-data-about-sleep-and-activity-nee)).
  - For us: **tension flows outward**, to the station administration, the outsiders and the
    newcomer (the **player**). People under ICE stress blame "them up there" before they blame
    their neighbours. That gives a dependable source of both friction and solidarity.
- **Individual differences dominate.**
  - In Mars-500, **two crewmembers accounted for 85% of perceived conflicts**. One reported
    depression in 93% of mission weeks, while two others showed no disturbance over 17 months.
  - For us: stress reactions should be **concentrated in susceptible individuals** (high N, low
    stress tolerance, insecure attachment), not spread evenly across everyone.
- **Capsule habitats: Suedfeld & Steel 2000, *Annual Review of Psychology*.** This is the review
  of how environments like these are lived in ([Suedfeld's list](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~psuedfeld/EUERefs.html)).
- **The overview effect and Earth out of view.**
  - Astronauts report awe and self-transcendence at the sight of Earth: unity, connectedness,
    vastness ([Yaden et al. 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/David-Yaden/publication/298786174_The_Overview_Effect_Awe_and_Self-Transcendent_Experience_in_Space_Flight/links/570cf7a308ae2b772e42a6dd/The-Overview-Effect-Awe-and-Self-Transcendent-Experience-in-Space-Flight.pdf)).
  - Kanas & Manzey expect the opposite, **"Earth out of view"**, to deepen isolation.
  - For us: some residents are moved by the view (`C.awe`); some are homesick for a planet they may
    never have seen (`C.earthsick`).

## 3.3 The 1975 NASA settlement study (SP-413): the closest thing to a brief for Columbia
The Stanford torus design study sized a 10,000-person rotating habitat
([NASA SP-413](https://nss.org/settlement/nasa/75SummerStudy/s.s.doc.html)). Its human-factors
chapter and psychological appendix are directly relevant ([chapter 3](https://nss.org/settlement/nasa/75SummerStudy/Chapt3.html); [appendix A](https://nss.org/settlement/nasa/75SummerStudy/3appendA.html)):
- **Variety prevents stress.** "Variety, diversity, flexibility and motivation can make apparently
  deficient environments quite satisfactory to their inhabitants." It asks for large vistas,
  living things and changeable spaces, to avoid "motel banality". The criterion is at least
  **40 m² of projected area per inhabitant**.
- **Solipsism syndrome.** It "can be easily produced in an environment where everything is
  artificial, where everything is like a theater stage". It is countered by distant views,
  unpredictability and growing things.
- **Three models of social organization:**
  - **A, hierarchical:** efficiency, majority rule, homogeneity; similar residents grouped;
  - **B, individualistic:** self-contained dwellings, privacy, little interdependence;
  - **C, heterogenistic:** symbiosis through diversity; ages, occupations and family types mixed
    for mutual support.
- **The matching problem.** "Each culture is healthy for those whose tastes, abilities and rate of
  communication match with it, and unhealthy for others". So there should be several community
  types, with variety inside each.
- **Selection and retention.** Large money incentives attract unsuitable settlers. People recruited
  temporarily rather than committed suffer *shimanagashi syndrome*, the distress of the exiled.
- **Small size.** Ten thousand people cannot be self-sufficient; cities need 100,000–200,000 for a
  full range of services. That means trade, communications and the option to go back.

**For us:**
- **Every settlement gets a social-organization type:** `W.org` = hierarchical, individualistic or
  heterogenistic, from its founding record.
- **Every person gets a fit to it:** `C.fit` = how well their values match that type. Poor fit
  raises stress.
- **Motive for coming** feeds solipsism and exile distress: committed people fare better than those
  who came for the money.

## 3.4 Tokens
**Settlement culture (`W.`)**, one set per settlement, derived from its record (age, founding
economy, remoteness, density, threat):
| token | meaning | basis |
|---|---|---|
| `W.age` | years since founding; the frontier-experience analogue | Bazzi et al. |
| `W.indiv` | individualism ↔ collectivism | TFE, voluntary settlement, low-interdependence economy |
| `W.honor` | insult and reputation sensitivity | mobile wealth plus weak policing (Nisbett & Cohen) |
| `W.tight` | norm strength and punishment | threat ecology (Gelfand); rises locally near life-support work |
| `W.relmob` | relational mobility | turnover and growth vs closed farming (Thomson et al.) |
| `W.org` | hierarchical, individualistic or heterogenistic | NASA SP-413 |
| `W.legacy` | trait-mean shifts from the founding economy (e.g. heavy industry: C −, N +) | Obschonka et al. |
| `W.outgroup` | who absorbs displaced tension (the administration, a rival settlement, newcomers) | Kanas; Mars-500 |
| `W.role*` | the roles the settlement needs (next research round) | slot |

**Personal adaptation (`C.`)**:
| token | meaning | basis |
|---|---|---|
| `C.origin` | born here, first-generation settler, recent arrival or contract worker | voluntary settlement; selection and retention |
| `C.motive` | why they (or their family) came: choice, money, escape, assigned | SP-413 (commitment protects) |
| `C.fit` | value fit to `W.org` and `W.tight` | the matching problem |
| `C.isol` | isolation tolerance | low N, high att_avoid-secure, introversion helps |
| `C.solip` | solipsism / unreality risk | artificiality exposure × low O-fantasy grounding; countered by nature access |
| `C.earthsick` / `C.awe` | homesick for Earth, or moved by the view | overview effect; Earth out of view |
| `C.displace` | tendency to push tension outward | ICE displacement |

**Generation.** Settlement tokens come first. A person's trait draw then gets small mean shifts
from `W.legacy`, `W.indiv` (O and E up slightly, A down slightly for individualist frontiers) and
their origin. The frontier effect is population-level and modest, so individuals still vary
widely.
