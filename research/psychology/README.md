# Psychology and relationships: the base of the story engine

This research is for the **story engine**. When anyone acts (above all the player), people react
throughout the station, including people several degrees of separation away, each in their own
way. Every character is still self-contained and generated on contact. Characters' personalities
set how they react. The end product is a compact set of **tokens** per character and situation.
From these the game builds the prompt that Groq turns into dialogue.

The user's brief (2026-09-30):
- personality types and personality disorders;
- scholarly work on frontier and space living;
- interpersonal relationships and degrees of separation;
- tokenize all of it, including each character's role in the world;
- research how to track it and generate it procedurally.

Roles in the world are the next research round. The framework leaves a slot for them (the
`W.role*` tokens).

Prototype: `tools/story/propagation_proto.py`. It builds a settlement from social foci, spreads
events by word of mouth, and shows that the lazy per-person query exactly matches a full
simulation (0 disagreements across 600 people), with realistic spread curves.

## The framework in one paragraph
- **Events.** Anything that matters (by the player, or a notable world event) is written to an
  append-only **event log**, stored as tokens (who, did what, to whom, how much, who saw).
- **What people know.** Nobody else is stored. When the player meets someone, the engine
  **computes** what they know: it searches back through the settlement's social graph, which is
  itself generated on demand from households, workplaces, streets, classes and clubs. Every
  "would this person pass it on, and when" decision is a hash of fixed ids, so the answer is the
  same every time. By Kempe, Kleinberg & Tardos's live-edge equivalence, it also agrees exactly
  with what a full simulation of everyone would have produced.
- **Distortion.** Word of mouth loses detail, sharpens the vivid part and bends toward what the
  hearer expects, as Allport & Postman found.
- **Reaction.** The hearer appraises what they know through their goals, standards and ties to
  the people involved (OCC appraisal, Weiner's attributions, balance theory). Their personality,
  values, attachment and the settlement's culture shape the result. That gives an emotion, an
  opinion of the player and a stance, chosen by their personal if-then signature (CAPS).
- **Dialogue.** The whole bundle compiles into a short, fixed-order token prompt that says what
  the character knows, feels and wants. Groq supplies only the words, and a validator stops it
  inventing knowledge the character doesn't have.
