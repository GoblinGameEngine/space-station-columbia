# What future buildings will look like (2026-10-05)

The station's game year is 2752, but its towns keep the founders' Great Lakes look (the bible). These trends cover
what is being built now and where research expects building to go: the "newest band" for the towns, and the source
for the parked *modern subdivisions* feature (research/buildings/modern_buildings_and_subdivisions.md).

## Residential

| Trend | Evidence | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy-driven form** (Passive House and IECC tightening; all-electric) | The 2021 IECC raised attic insulation to R-60 in zones 4-8 and tightened windows. Passive House in zones 5-7 needs care: too much south glass overheats in shoulder seasons; mini-split heat pumps are among the most cost-effective measures | **Compact, simple massing** (fewer corners and jogs), thick walls (deep window reveals), moderate windows concentrated south, exterior shading, no chimney, a heat-pump unit on a pad at the side, rooftop solar |
| **Prefab, modular and kit (again)** | Factory-built volumes and panels (DfMA) for cost and quality | Box-module proportions (about 12-16 ft wide modules), repeated units, flat or low-slope roofs, crisp panel joints: a modern *catalogue house*, echoing Sears (02 §2) |
| **3D-printed houses** | Printed concrete walls in the single-family market for "mass customisation" and less site labour | **Ribbed, layered concrete walls**, curved corners, 1 storey, a timber or metal roof on top |
| **Mass timber** | The 2021 IBC adds Types IV-A, IV-B and IV-C (tall timber up to 18 storeys). CLT and glulam cut construction carbon by up to about 45 % | Mid-rise apartments with **visible wood** soffits, balconies and lobbies, larger windows, and expressed grid structure |
| **Missing middle and ADUs** | Zoning reform toward duplexes, fourplexes, cottage courts and backyard cottages (urban_layout 01) | House-scaled multi-unit buildings, and small rear cottages over garages on old lots |
| **Single-stair "point access" blocks** | Reform letting 4-6 storeys use one stair (US cities following Europe) | **Narrower, deeper, windows on 3-4 sides**, small footprints on single lots, instead of corridor slabs |
| **Builder suburbs today** (from the parked research: Lennar and Toll Brothers models) | — | Stacked rectangular volumes at 2-3 roof heights, about 3 materials (stucco or fibre-cement field, a dark metal accent, a little wood or stone), large frameless glass on living sides, deep overhangs, a garage-forward front |

## Commercial

| Trend | Evidence | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Dark stores and micro-fulfilment** | Former shops become pick-and-pack centres (3,000-20,000 sq ft) close to homes; pandemic demand accelerated this | **Blank or frosted shopfronts**, loading doors where windows were, delivery vans and pickup lockers, a small branded pickup counter |
| **Hybrid and experiential stores** | Stores combine showroom, pickup and fulfilment | Fewer, smaller stores with big glazed showrooms, curbside pickup bays and signed lanes |
| **Dead malls and strips reused** | About 25 % of US malls were forecast to close within 5 years (2020); the leading reuses are multifamily housing, fulfilment and last-mile hubs | Apartments over or beside old mall boxes, parking fields infilled with blocks; a strip's anchor box turned into a fulfilment hub or a church |
| **Office to residential** | Adaptive reuse costs 15-20 % less than new building; cities fast-track it | Old offices with new balconies and operable windows, and lobby retail |
| **The 5-over-1 mixed-use block** | IBC Type III/V over a Type I podium (01 §3) | 5-6 storeys, a retail podium, panelised facades in 2-4 colours, bays and balconies, applied "plank" accents. The default new main-street building since about 2005 |

## For the station (a bible question)

- **The research's direction**: tighter envelopes, factory building, timber and printed walls, multi-unit buildings
  at house scale, and retail that turns into logistics and reuse.
- **SSC's newest band** has two plausible readings:
  1. **The founders' "future"**: 2250s modern, as the parked modern-subdivision feature imagines.
  2. **Built aboard**: the station's own construction culture after 500 years. Factory modules, printed walls and
     reused older buildings fit a closed world with limited materials.
- The bible should say which. The research supports both, and they can coexist as different bands.
