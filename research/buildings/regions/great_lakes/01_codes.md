# Building codes: Great Lakes region (2026-10-05)

**Region**: the founders' Great Lakes states and provinces (research/bible/01_world.md): Ohio, Michigan, Indiana,
Illinois, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Lake Erie Pennsylvania and western New York, and Ontario.

**Why codes matter to a building generator**: codes are a *hidden geometry*. They decide where a window may go, how
tall a sill is, how steep a stair is, how close two houses may stand and what a wall is made of. A generated building
that breaks them looks wrong even to people who have never read a code. This file lists the provisions that **change
what a building looks like**, not the whole code.

Values are the current model codes (2018-2021 IRC/IBC/IECC) as the states adopt them. Numbers marked † are from
memory of the 2021 IBC tables and should be checked against the code text before they are used as hard limits.

## 2. House provisions that shape the look (IRC; the RCO and MRC follow it)

| Provision | Rule | What it does to the building |
|---|---|---|
| R305 ceiling height | Habitable rooms at least 7 ft (2.13 m); beams may project to 6 ft 4 in | Storey height of about 8-9 ft floor to floor in new houses. The 1890s house's 9-10 ft ceilings are period, not code |
| R304 room size | Habitable rooms at least 70 sq ft and 7 ft in any dimension | The smallest bedroom box |
| R303 light and ventilation | Glazing of at least 8 % of the floor area per habitable room; openable area of at least 4 % (or mechanical ventilation) | Every habitable room has a window; a 120 sq ft bedroom needs about 10 sq ft of glass: one or two ordinary windows |
| R310 emergency escape | Every sleeping room (and basement) has an opening of at least 5.7 sq ft net (5.0 at grade), 24 in high clear, 20 in wide clear, **sill at most 44 in** above the floor; window wells at least 9 sq ft with a ladder past 44 in deep | Bedroom windows are large and low: casements or tall double-hungs. **Basement egress wells**: a recognisable post-1990s feature |
| R311 egress door, stairs | Exit door 32 in clear (36 x 80 in leaf typical); stairs **riser at most 7¾ in, tread at least 10 in**, headroom 6 ft 8 in, width 36 in; a landing at each exterior door | About 14 risers per 9 ft storey, so a straight stair runs about 11-12 ft in plan. A front stoop of 1-3 risers |
| R312 guards | 36 in guard where the drop is over 30 in; balusters under 4 in apart | Porches more than 30 in up have rails. Older low porches had none |
| R302.1 fire separation | Exterior walls closer than 5 ft to the lot line are fire-rated (1 hr); openings are not allowed under 3 ft, and limited to 25 % of the wall at 3-5 ft; projections (eaves) at least 2 ft from the line | **Side walls of tight lots have few or no windows**; eaves stop short of the line; zero-lot-line houses have a blank side |
| R302.2 townhouses | A fire separation (two 1-hr walls or a common 2-hr wall, 1-hr with sprinklers) to the roof sheathing, or a parapet | Townhouse rows show the party walls at the roofline, sometimes as parapets |
| R302.5-6 garage | Gypsum between garage and house; a self-closing solid door | (Internal; it pushes garages to the side or front, with a fire-rated wall) |
| R403.1.4 frost | Footings below the frost line: about 32-36 in in southern Ohio and Indiana, 42 in in Michigan (most), 48 in in Wisconsin, 60-80 in in Minnesota | **Full basements are the norm** (if you dig to 42-60 in for footings, the basement is nearly free), so houses sit 2-3 ft above grade with steps up to the door |
| R301.2 snow | Ground snow 20-30 psf in most of Ohio and Indiana, 30-60 psf+ downwind of the lakes (lake-effect belts: NE Ohio, NW Pennsylvania, western NY, west Michigan, the UP) | Steeper roofs and heavier rafters in snow belts; the 8:12-12:12 gable is common; low-slope roofs need more structure |
| R905 roof slopes | Asphalt shingles need at least 2:12 (2:12-4:12 with double underlayment); wood shingle and slate 3:12-4:12; low-slope needs membrane | Shingled roofs are almost never flatter than 4:12. Flat roofs (membrane) mean commercial or modern |
| N1102 energy (IECC) | Climate zone 5A for most of the region, 6A for northern Michigan, Wisconsin and southern Minnesota, 7 for the far north. About R-20 or 13+5 walls, R-49-60 ceilings, window U ≤ 0.30 | **2x6 walls** (6½-7 in thick plus siding) in new houses; deeper window reveals; bigger attics with raised-heel trusses; window-to-wall ratios held near 15-20 % in practice |

## 3. Commercial and multifamily provisions that shape the look (IBC)

| Provision | Rule | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Construction types (Ch. 6) | I and II noncombustible (concrete, steel), III masonry or noncombustible outer walls with a wood interior, IV heavy timber / mass timber, V all wood | **Main Street's brick fronts are Type III**: masonry outside, wood joists inside. Strip centres are Type IIB (steel and block) or VB |
| Heights and areas (Tables 504.3, 504.4, 506.2) | Height and area per storey by occupancy group x construction type; more with sprinklers and with frontage (open sides) | **Type caps produce typical buildings**: R-2 apartments in VA wood frame are at most 3 storeys unsprinklered or 4 sprinklered (60 ft)†; IIIA reaches 5 sprinklered (85 ft)†. Hence the **"5-over-1"**: 4-5 storeys of wood frame (III or V) on a 1-2 storey concrete podium (IA) of shops and parking. M (retail) in VB is 1 storey, 9,000 sq ft† unsprinklered: the small one-storey shop |
| Mixed occupancy and fire walls (Ch. 5, 706) | Fire walls divide buildings into separate buildings for height and area purposes; a parapet at least 30 in above the roof | **The rhythm of party walls and parapets along a commercial block**; a tall building is often several buildings |
| Exterior wall openings (Table 705.8) | Unprotected openings are limited by fire separation distance (none at 0-3 ft, 10-15 % at 3-5 ft, growing with distance) | **Blank side walls on lot-line commercial buildings**: painted signs ("ghost signs") go on them |
| Means of egress (Ch. 10) | Two exits above about 3 storeys or by occupant load; exit stairs enclosed | Double-loaded corridors with **a stair at each end**: the long slab apartment block. Single-stair reform (some cities now allow 4-6 storeys on one stair) allows narrower, deeper "point access" blocks with windows on more sides |
| Accessibility (Ch. 11, ANSI A117.1, ADA 1990) | An accessible entrance, ramps at most 1:12, accessible parking | **Zero-step storefronts and lobby entries** after about 1990; ramps added to old fronts; the van-accessible stall by the door |
| Sprinklers (903) | Required by occupancy and size; trade-offs for height, area and separation | Riser rooms and fire department connections on facades; larger glass allowances |

## 4. Code history: what each era's rules left on the buildings

This matters because we generate *era bands* (research/urban_layout, decision 1). Each band's buildings must obey the
rules *of their era*, not today's.

| Era | Rule or event | Visible result |
|---|---|---|
| 1600s-1800s | Colonial "building acts": no wood roofs in town | — |
| After 1871 (the Chicago fire) | **Fire limits**: masonry required within a drawn downtown boundary | **Brick downtowns, frame neighbourhoods**: the clean material edge at the fire limit is a regional signature |
| 1867-1909 | Tenement laws (NYC 1867, 1879, **1901**; **Chicago 1902**): light and air in every room, toilets, fire escapes, minimum room and ceiling sizes | Light courts, air shafts, **external iron fire escapes** on pre-1930 apartments and upper floors of commercial blocks |
| 1903-1911 | Iroquois Theatre (Chicago 1903), **Collinwood School (Cleveland 1908)**, Triangle (NYC 1911) fires | Multiple exits, outward-swinging doors, panic hardware; **1910s-20s schools with two stairs and many doors** |
| 1905 | National Board of Fire Underwriters' *Recommended (later National) Building Code* | Standardisation; masonry party walls with parapets |
| 1915 / 1927 / 1940 | BOCA founded (its code in 1950, used in the Midwest and Northeast); the Uniform Building Code 1927 (West); the Standard Building Code 1945 (South); the NFPA *Building Exits Code* 1927 | Regional code families: **the Great Lakes were BOCA country** until 2000 |
| 1934-1950s | FHA Minimum Property Standards | The uniform postwar small house: 1-storey, 24-28 ft deep, standard windows, a basement in the north |
| 1961-1990 | Accessibility: the University of Illinois's 1961 specification, ABA 1968, ANSI A117.1 1980, **ADA 1990** | Ramps, level entries, wide doors after 1990 |
| 1970s-80s | Energy codes after the oil shocks (Model Energy Code 1983 → IECC 1998) | Smaller windows, 2x6 walls, vinyl replacement windows on older houses |
| 2000 | The International Building and Residential Codes replace BOCA, UBC and SBC | National uniformity |
| 2021 | IBC Types IV-A, IV-B and IV-C (tall mass timber, up to 18 storeys) | Exposed timber mid-rises (04_futures.md) |

Sources: [NPS Preservation Brief 51: building codes for historic buildings](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1739/upload/preservation-brief-51-building-codes.pdf);
[A history of US building codes (Fine Homebuilding)](https://finehomebuilding.com/?p=512186);
[A short history of building codes (Mother Earth News)](https://www.motherearthnews.com/green-homes/building-codes-zmaz06djzraw);
[Fire limits (Encyclopedia of Chicago)](https://www.encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org/pages/457.html);
[Tenement House Act 1901 (NYC archaeology timeline)](https://archaeology.cityofnewyork.us/collection/nyc-timeline/tenement-house-act);

## 5. What the generator takes from this (rules to encode)

1. **Window placement** follows the code envelope.
   - At least 8 % of each habitable room's floor area is glass, and every bedroom has an egress window with its sill
     at most 44 in.
   - Walls within 3 ft of a lot line have no openings; walls 3-5 ft away get at most 25 %.
   - Pre-1950 houses obey the *period* rules instead: large double-hungs at a 24-30 in sill, and no egress wells.
2. **Floor-to-floor heights** by era: about 10-12 ft before 1900, 9 ft in 1900-40, 8 ft 1¼ in (8 ft ceilings) after
   1945, and 9 ft ceilings again after 2000.
3. **Foundation exposure** by frost line: houses sit 18-36 in above grade, with 2-4 risers to the door.
4. **Roof pitch**: shingled roofs at least 4:12, mostly 6:12-12:12 in the north; flat roofs only on commercial,
   Type III blocks or modern work.
5. **Commercial construction type sets the form**:
   - masonry Type III blocks of 2-4 storeys within the old fire limits;
   - 1-storey Type IIB/VB boxes on the strip;
   - 5-over-1s only in post-2005 bands.
6. **Party walls and parapets** at the fire-wall rhythm, 20-25 ft on Main Street, with blank side walls carrying
   painted signs.
7. **Accessibility after 1990**: zero-step commercial entries, ramps retrofitted onto older fronts, and accessible
   parking stalls.
8. **Station overlay**: the station's own code body is a bible decision. The research suggests the founders brought
   these codes aboard (they came from the NAIC states), and 500 years aboard changed them. Fire limits and egress
   matter *more* in a sealed hull.
