# 2. How often people break the rules of the road: national and Ohio rates

What the population's driving weights are calibrated to. "Measured" figures come from the
sources listed. "Estimate" marks a parameter with no direct measurement found; it is set by
analogy and labelled as such.

## Speeding (measured)

**NHTSA, *National Survey of Speeding Attitudes and Behaviors 2011* (DOT HS 811 865):**
6,000 drivers, weighted to the US population.
- **Driver types:** 30% nonspeeders, 40% sometime speeders, 30% speeders.
- **By age:** 50% of drivers aged 16–20 are speeders, against 15% of those 65 and older.
  Nonspeeders are 38% of drivers over 55 and 17% of those 20 or younger.
- **By sex:** men are 36% speeders, women 28%. Nonspeeders: men 25%, women 32%.
- **By income:** speeders are 42% of drivers with household incomes over $100,000.
- **How far over, and how often:**

  | How often they report driving… | Often | Sometimes | Rarely | Never |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 15 mph over on multi-lane divided highways | 4.9% | 14.2% | 33.2% | 47.7% |
  | 15 mph over on two-lane highways | 2.3% | 7.5% | 25.9% | 64.2% |
  | 10 mph over on neighbourhood streets | 2.8% | 7.9% | 24.8% | 64.4% |

- **Keeping up with traffic:** 44.8% "keep up with the faster traffic"; 34.9% "stay with
  slower-moving traffic".
- **Enforcement:** 9% were stopped for speeding in the past year (20% of speeders, 4–5% of
  the rest). Drivers ticketed in the past year think they can go 10.6 mph over before being
  ticketed.

**NHTSA *Traffic Safety Facts: Speeding, 2022 data* (DOT HS 813 582), crashes:**
- 29% of traffic deaths were in speeding-related crashes; 18% of drivers in fatal crashes
  were speeding.
- Among 15–20-year-olds in fatal crashes, 35% of male and 19% of female drivers were speeding.
- Speeding drivers in fatal crashes, compared with non-speeding drivers:
  - 29% had no valid licence (vs 15%);
  - 38% had a BAC of .08 or more (vs 18%).

**Ohio, from the same 2022 fact sheet:**
- 1,275 traffic deaths, 271 speeding-related (21%, against 29% nationally).
- 204 of 1,364 passenger-vehicle drivers in fatal crashes were speeding (15%, against 18%
  nationally).
- 40% of speeding drivers in fatal crashes were alcohol-impaired, against 23% of non-speeding
  drivers.
- **Ohio weight:** 15/18 ≈ 0.83, applied to national prevalence. Ohio's speeding involvement is
  about five-sixths of the national rate.

## Rolling through stop signs (measured, with small samples)

- **DeVeauuse, Kim & Peek-Asa (1999), *J. American College Health*:** at three stop-sign
  crosswalks on a large urban campus, **22.8 per 100 vehicles came to a complete stop**
  (1.4/100 bicycles, 46.2/100 commuter vans). This rose to **53 per 100 when pedestrians were
  in the crosswalk**.
- **Van Houten & Retting (2001), *J. Applied Behavior Analysis*:** complete stops were well
  short of universal at three crash-prone stop-sign intersections, and rose when an animated
  LED "look both ways" sign prompted drivers.
- **Mounce (1981), TRR:** 2,830 observations at 66 low-volume intersections. Violation rates
  varied with traffic on the major road and with sight distance: fewer violations where cross
  traffic was heavier.
- **Feest (1968), *Law & Society Review*:** a classic observation of stop-sign behaviour.
  Compliance depends on the situation, such as whether others are present.
- **Model:** about **25–30% of drivers make a full stop when nobody is around**. More stop when
  pedestrians or cross traffic are present (the 53% figure). Most of the rest slow to a walking
  pace rather than drive straight through.

## Red lights (measured, crashes)

**Retting, Ulmer & Williams (1999), *Accident Analysis & Prevention*:**
- About 40% of crashes happen at intersections.
- Red-light runners in crashes were more likely than other drivers to be under 30 and male,
  and to have prior moving violations, drunk-driving convictions and invalid licences.

## Passing on the right; following too closely (estimates)

- **Passing on the right:** no prevalence measurement was found. The law allows it in the two
  cases of ORC 4511.28; the violation is passing off the roadway or on a narrow street.
  **Estimate:** a violator passes when the chance arises at about the same rate they run a
  stop, around 10% of opportunities across the population.
- **Tailgating:** observational headway studies exist (Michael, Leeming & Dwyer 2000,
  *Transportation Research Part F*), but no abstract was retrievable. **Estimate:** desired
  time headway centred at 1.5 s, s.d. 0.4 s, floor 0.6 s. Under 1 s counts as tailgating,
  about 10% of drivers.
- **Failing to yield to pedestrians:** the campus study's 53% stopping rate with pedestrians
  present means **about 47% failed to stop**. The model gives a person on the road directly
  ahead a hard stop from everyone, since no one hits them. The violation is the softer
  failure: passing close in front of or behind a person, instead of stopping for one about to
  cross.

## Base rate for wider offending (measured)

- **Shannon, Uggen & Schnittker (2017), *Demography*:** people with felony convictions are
  **8% of US adults**, and 3% have been to prison.
- The study reports higher rates for Black men, a product of policing and sentencing
  structures. **The game uses no race or ethnicity input**; propensity comes from individual
  factors (03).
