## Everyday customs
- **the long goodbye:** leaving takes half an hour: goodbyes in the kitchen, at the door, on the porch and at the car
- **porch sitting:** summer evenings on the porch, waving at walkers
- **euchre night:** the card game of the Great Lakes; leagues in taverns and lodges; bragging rights ('going alone')
- **the potluck:** everyone brings a dish; the church basement supper
- **casserole duty:** a dish to any family with a birth, an illness or a Return
- **the parish festival:** summer weekend: rides, beer tent, raffles, polka
- **Friday is fish:** most families eat fish on Friday; taverns full
- **lake days:** summer Sundays at the beach or the lake
- **the Steward nod:** a small nod to a passing tender (maintenance machine); older people touch a hatch for luck
- **the dusk hush:** at the Sunline's dimming, some older people stop talking for a moment
- **facing north:** beds and pews are often set to face north ('the way we're going') -- Waymakers insist, others just do
- **not asking about shares:** asking a childless adult 'why no kids?' is rude; people say 'they gifted' and leave it
- **pop, not soda:** calling it 'soda' marks you as 'Carrow French' or a snob
- **the tailgate:** spinball game days: cookouts from the backs of vans
- **the rummage sale:** spring and autumn: whole streets sell their old things
- **the garage:** men and women alike keep a workshop or a bar in the garage
- **the church supper:** Wednesday or Sunday evening
- **the union hall dance:** Saturday nights in Pelican Cove and Tern Harbor
- **lodge night:** the Order of the Lamp meets Tuesdays
- **the Wire evening:** families gather for 'The Lamplighters' at 19:30 on Thursdays
- **Draw Day:** the first of the month: the Draw's credits land; grocery lines; small indulgences
- **Bank luck:** people from Bank-born families are thought lucky: touch their sleeve before a game

## Superstitions
- never block a tender's path: bad luck for a year
- knocking on a sealed hatch wakes 'downstairs'; on Halloween it's a dare
- sleep with your head to the north for good dreams
- since VY 498 some keep a candle by the bed 'in case the Sunline blinks'
- tear your Share Day letter's corner for luck; keep the corner in your wallet
- touch a Bank-born person's sleeve before a game
- no meat on Friday or the nets come up empty (fishing towns)
- the empty chair at Launch Day dinner must not be sat in
- throw a coin in the Falls pool for a safe year
- don't build below the Flood Line
- don't whistle at the Sunline's dimming
- say Minnie's Line when you pass a Return hall

## Etiquette
- never arrive empty-handed; bring a dish or a six-pack of pop
- offer food three times; refuse twice before accepting (Midwest and Mexican manners agree)
- shoes off indoors in most homes
- wave to drivers on the country roads (the one-finger wave)
- never ask why someone is childless
- thank the cook by name at the fish fry
- dress up for church and Returns
- people say 'thank you' to the Boards, half joking
- walk your guests to their pod
- the host eats last

## Taboos
- arguing to turn the ship back (Charter art. 21): not illegal to say, but social suicide (strength 0.9)
- selling a share (a crime since VY 466) (strength 1.0)
- breaking into the Undercroft (the Bellhaven Five are half heroes, half criminals) (strength 0.8)
- praising Silas Brandvold in public (strength 0.7)
- wasting water ('the Balance') -- running taps are scolded (strength 0.6)
- joking about the Kettle Flood in Cedar Ford or Dunmore Crossing (strength 0.8)
- joking about the Gray Fever near the old (strength 0.7)
- a Pike in Tern Harbor (strength 0.9)
- saying 'soda' in Harrow Falls (a light taboo) (strength 0.1)

## Dialect

Inland Northern American English (the Great Lakes), levelled with the refuge's Southern American, African American and Latino Englishes over 500 years

- **phonology:** the Northern Cities Vowel Shift half-kept: 'bag' like 'beg-ag', 'lot' fronted; Port Carrow and Tern Harbor: stronger shift, dropped /r/ in old speakers; South Shore: Spanish-influenced rhythm and vowels ('Point English'); Haskins Corner and the countryside: Southern-flavoured drawl, 'y'all'; the young everywhere: a levelled 'Wire accent' from the radio
- **grammar:** 'needs washed' (Pittsburgh/Ohio); 'anymore' meaning 'nowadays' in positive sentences ('Anymore everyone takes a stat'); 'come with' ('Do you want to come with?'); 'you guys' / 'youse' / 'y'all' plural; Spanish tags on the South Shore ('¿verdad?', 'pues')
- **register:** Kessler: formal, lawyerly; Bellhaven: academic slang; Port Carrow: clipped and proud; Solana Point: loud, bilingual, fast

## Schooling

- **structure:** school from 5 to 18 in every town; Columbia College at Bellhaven for about one in six
- **history_taught:** Launch Day and Ada Carrow; the Charter; Tamsin, the first child; the Burn Fever; the Balance Act; the Last Earthborn; the Troubles and the Reconciliation; the Kettle Flood; the Gray Fever
- **history_skipped:** the Earth-link after VY 300; how the station works; where exactly the ship is going (taught as 'the Far Shore', a paragraph); the Returners' arguments
- **science:** biology, farming, fishing, chemistry of the kitchen, basic electricity; 'station science' (what the Steward does) as a list, not an explanation
- **civics:** the Charter, the Assembly, the shares
