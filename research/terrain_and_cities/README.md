# Terrain, coasts, hill cities and the 1:1 towns (research, 2026-10-07)

The user's brief, 2026-10-07: tall rocky hills and smooth rolling hills around the map, with Calder kept flat. Harrow
Falls is a hilly city with a natural waterfall fed by a spring on a tall rocky hill it is built around. The North Sea
shore is rocky with towns on protected harbors in a New England style. The South Sea shore is smooth and sandy with few
rocky beaches and several natural harbors, in a California style with tile roofs and stucco walls. The coastal styles
blend into the Great Lakes style toward the river. Roads are graded properly. The large cities get apartments and lively
centres. Every settlement over 9,000 people gets its own character and landmarks.

## 1. Road grades: the rules we build to

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| Caltrans Highway Design Manual, Index 204.3, Table 204.3 (maximum grades) | Freeways and expressways: level 3%, rolling 4%, mountainous 6%. Rural highways: 4 / 5 / 7%. Urban highways: 6 / 7 / 9%. Ramps 8%. Minimum grade 0.3% (0.5% in snow) for drainage. |
| Caltrans HDM 204.4 (vertical curves) | At ≥ 2% grade change and ≥ 40 mph, minimum curve length = 10 × design speed in feet. Otherwise at least 200 ft. No curve needed for ≤ 0.5%. Grade breaks no closer than 50 ft. |
| Caltrans HDM 204.5 (sustained grades) | Investigate a climbing lane where the upgrade is over 2% and the total rise over 250 ft. |
| California Fire Code (CFC 503.2.7) and local amendments (Gilroy, Sonoma, Woodland) | Fire apparatus access roads: maximum 15–16%. Steeper needs the fire chief's approval and sprinklered houses. |
| Hillside subdivision ordinances (Calabasas, Los Gatos, Santa Rosa, San Carlos, Lakeport) | Local streets up to 15%, or 15–20% for runs of at most 200 ft. **10% maximum on switchbacks and curves under 200 ft radius.** Streets follow the contours. On steep slopes a street splits into two one-way terraces, half cut and half fill. Sidewalks and parking may be dropped to reduce grading. Average lot slope ≤ 25%. A slope-density formula lowers density as slope rises. |
| AASHTO terrain classes | Level < 2%, rolling 2–8%, mountainous > 8%. |
| Real extremes | Pittsburgh's Canton Avenue is 37%, the steepest street in the US. Pittsburgh's public stairways reach about 70% and are legally *streets* ("paper streets"). |

**Applied to the game.** The road profiler grades each road class to these limits:
- US 30 and highways: ≤ 6%.
- County and section roads: ≤ 8%.
- Town streets and main streets: ≤ 10–12%, with main streets kept under 6% (a downtown wants level frontage).
- Residential streets in hill towns: ≤ 15%, up to 20% for short runs. Switchbacks ≤ 10%.

Where a straight street would exceed its limit, the street follows the contour or switches back. Where even that fails,
a **public stair** replaces the street link, Pittsburgh and Seattle style.

## 2. How hill cities are laid out (Seattle, Pittsburgh, Cincinnati, Duluth)

- **Seattle.**
  - The 1880s grids were laid straight over the hills.
  - The city engineer R. H. Thomson regraded the worst of them, 1897–1930. The Denny Hill regrade lowered it by more
    than 100 ft. The Jackson regrade cut 85 ft and took a 15% street down to under 5%.
  - Where a grid street was still too steep it stopped, and **a stairway carried the connection**.
  - On a side slope the street **splits into upper and lower one-way terraces**.
- **Pittsburgh.**
  - The flat land along the rivers went to industry (the mills). Workers lived on the hilltops and hillsides.
  - The city has about 700–800 public stairways, mostly built 1940s–50s, linking hill neighbourhoods to the mills and
    streetcar lines.
  - Neighbourhoods are islands on ridges and benches, separated by wooded slopes too steep to build.
- **Cincinnati.**
  - The dense **basin** (downtown, Over-the-Rhine) filled first.
  - Then streetcars and **five inclined-plane railways** carried the city up onto the hilltops (Mount Adams, Mount
    Auburn, Clifton, Price Hill, Fairview). Each became a distinct hilltop neighbourhood with views over the basin.
  - The hills both separate and define the neighbourhoods.
- **Duluth.** This is the closest match to Harrow Falls: a lake city built on a 700 ft hill rising from Lake Superior.
  - It is long and thin along the shore.
  - Streets run parallel to the lake. The avenues run straight up the slope; some climb steeply and others switch back.
  - The Seventh Avenue Incline (1891–1939) climbed 509 ft in about half a mile.
  - Enger Tower is a stone observation tower on a hilltop.

**The pattern we use for a hill town:**
1. A dense, level **basin or terrace downtown** on the flattest ground by the water.
2. **Contour streets** in benches above it.
3. **Fall-line avenues** straight up the slope where they stay within their limit, switchbacks where they don't, and
   **stairs** where neither works.
4. **Hilltop neighbourhoods** with the big houses and views.
5. **Wooded slopes** left unbuilt where the slope is over about 30%.
6. One **incline railway** in a big hill town.
7. **Industry on the flats.**

## 3. The North Sea shore: rocky New England

- **Geology (Maine Geological Survey, USGS).**
  - It is a *drowned*, glaciated coast. Resistant granite forms headlands, peninsulas and islands; softer rock is
    eroded into coves and estuaries.
  - Old river valleys were flooded into deep channels and tidal inlets.
  - **Rocky headlands shelter small harbours and pocket beaches.** Glacial bluffs line the sheltered coves.
- **Towns.**
  - They sit on protected harbours behind headlands, with a working waterfront at the centre: wharves, lobster and
    fish piers, boatyards.
  - Narrow streets climb from the harbour. Buildings are close together.
  - There is granite everywhere (Stonington's quarries supplied Rockefeller Center).
- **Architecture** (Castine, Kennebunkport, Bath, Marblehead):
  - Cape Cod, saltbox, Federal and Greek Revival captains' houses;
  - Shingle Style summer houses;
  - white clapboard and weathered cedar shingle, brick for the commercial blocks;
  - steep gable roofs, white church steeples.
- **Town plan.** The New England **common or green**, with the meetinghouse (white church), town hall, library and
  big old houses around it, and Main Street leading to it. The harbour is the working heart.

## 4. The South Sea shore: sandy California

- **Coast.** Long sandy beaches, dunes and bluffs, with few rocky points. Natural harbours sit behind a headland or a
  rock: Morro Bay's 576 ft Morro Rock at the harbour mouth, the Dana Point headlands, and Monterey and Half Moon Bay in
  the lee of points. Long piers (Pismo's is 1,370 ft).
- **Architecture: Spanish Colonial Revival and Mission Revival.** Santa Barbara's El Pueblo Viejo guidelines, San
  Clemente's "Spanish Village by the Sea" deed rules (1925), the 1915 Panama-California Exposition.
  - **White or earth stucco walls, red clay barrel-tile roofs.**
  - Deep-set wood windows, wrought iron, arcades and loggias.
  - **Courtyards, patios, pergolas, paseos.**
  - Bell towers, Mission parapets and gables.
  - **Monterey Colonial**: two storeys with a cantilevered upper balcony along the front.
  - **Bungalow courts**: U-shaped groups of small stucco cottages around a garden.
  - Downtowns are **State Street**: arcaded blocks, paseos through to courtyards, plazas with fountains (El Paseo,
    La Arcada).
- **The transition inland.** Stucco and tile only in coastal towns and their near settlements. The share of
  tile and stucco falls with distance from the sea toward the river, where the Great Lakes / Midwest style (frame,
  clapboard, brick, asphalt shingle) takes over.

## 5. Cities of 30–45k in the Midwest, West and Upper South

- **Downtown.**
  - The traditional business district lies within about 0.5 mile of the centre (University of Wisconsin Extension's
    study of 259 Midwest cities of 25–75k).
  - Restaurants and bars now anchor the mix, with services (banks, insurance, law, medical), specialty retail and
    civic uses.
  - The healthy ones were regional centres, with grand public buildings.
- **Upper South and Midwest county seats.** The **courthouse square** (the "Shelbyville square", spread from central
  Tennessee): the courthouse stands alone in the centre, and business blocks face it on all four sides.
- **The West.** A gridded core around Main Street (Bozeman, Boise). Newer suburbs on curvilinear streets and
  cul-de-sacs.
- **Densities** (units per acre):

  | Type | Units per acre |
  |---|---|
  | Rowhouses and townhouses | 12–18 |
  | Mansion apartments and courtyard clusters | 15–22 |
  | Garden apartments | 19–25 |
  | 3–4-storey street-front apartments | 26–40 |
  | Downtown core | ~50 |

**For our cities:**
- A downtown of 3–6-storey blocks, with flats above shops and some mid-rise apartments.
- A ring of walk-up apartments and courts.
- Older neighbourhoods of houses and doubles.
- Postwar suburbs on curvilinear streets.
- An arterial strip with strip centres.
- Industry by the rail line or the water.

## 6. Waterfall cities (for Harrow Falls)

Paterson (the Great Falls of the Passaic, 77 ft), Spokane (the falls run through downtown), Little Falls NY (a gorge),
Cohoes ("Spindle City", the Harmony Mills), and Great Falls MT. The pattern:
- the town is founded at the falls for water power;
- **mills line a raceway** below the falls;
- the falls themselves become the downtown's park and landmark, with a footbridge or overlook;
- later the old mills become lofts and museums.

## 7. Landmarks and character, by settlement (over 9,000 people)

### Harrow Falls (41,800)

- **Character:** the lake-bluff Duluth of the station.
- **Terrain:** built around **Harrow Hill**, a tall granite knob. A spring near its top feeds **Harrow Run**, which
  drops over the hill's lake-facing cliff as **the Falls** into a gorge and then to Lake Tamsin.
- **Landmarks:**
  - **Falls Park**: an overlook, a footbridge across the gorge below the falls, and the old mill raceway with the
    Old Mill Lofts;
  - **the Harrow Hill Incline**, a funicular from downtown to the hilltop;
  - **Lookout Tower**, a stone observation tower on the summit;
  - city stairs;
  - the lake breakwater light and the pier.
- **Layout:** downtown on the lake terrace, contour streets climbing the hill, hilltop mansions.

### Port Carrow (38,600)

- **Character:** the Portland, Maine of the North Sea.
- **Terrain:** a rocky headland harbour.
- **Landmarks:**
  - the **Old Port**, brick and granite warehouse blocks;
  - the **Signal Tower** (an observatory like Portland's);
  - the **granite Custom House**;
  - the **Eastern Promenade** hill park;
  - the fish pier and wharves;
  - a lighthouse on the outer ledge;
  - a white-steepled First Parish church;
  - a town green.

### Solana Point (44,200)

- **Character:** the Santa Barbara / Dana Point / Huntington Beach of the South Sea.
- **Landmarks:**
  - **Mission Solana**, the old mission church with its bell tower;
  - a Spanish-style **county courthouse with a clock tower**;
  - an arcaded **Paseo** downtown with plazas and fountains;
  - the **long pier**;
  - the **harbour** behind its **headland**, with breakwater, marina and lighthouse;
  - a bluff-top park.

### Kessler (34,500)

- **Character:** a Midwest rail and manufacturing city on the flat plain.
- **Landmarks:**
  - **Union Station**, Art Deco with a clock tower;
  - the roundhouse and yards;
  - the **Kessler Works** with its smokestacks;
  - an Art Deco city hall;
  - the grain elevators;
  - **Sauk Park** with its pond;
  - the water tower;
  - a brick ballpark.
- **Layout:** a grid of rail-era blocks and a wide main street.

### Oceanview (9,800)

- **Character:** the Santa Cruz of the South Sea.
- **Landmarks:**
  - the **Beach Boardwalk** with a historic **carousel** (no roller coasters, by the coastal spec);
  - the **Casino** ballroom and arcade hall;
  - the municipal **wharf**;
  - a surf point with its lighthouse;
  - Mission-style hotels.

### Marlowe (9,100)

- **Character:** a county seat on an Upper South / Midwest **courthouse square**.
- **Landmarks:**
  - the domed **Brannock County Courthouse** standing alone on the square;
  - an opera house;
  - the water tower;
  - Carnegie library.

### The rest

Smaller towns keep their existing archetypes (Brightwater, Fenwick's college, Bellhaven, Tamarack...) and get
style by region. Calder is unchanged.
